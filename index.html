The Last-Mile Reality

The 4 Fatal Bottlenecks in Rural Indian Healthcare

Why 800 Million citizens wait until preventable symptoms turn into critical emergencies.

1. Doctor Deserts

India has 0.7 doctors per 1,000 people (vs WHO 1.0 standard). 80% of doctors reside in urban centers serving only 31% of the population.

Median Travel to PHC: 40–60 mins (3-4h in tribal zones)

2. Literacy Barrier

22 official languages, 780+ dialects. National portals default to English/Hindi. Rural female literacy is ~67% (<50% in Bihar/Rajasthan).

All major symptom apps: English Only

3. Bandwidth Void

Rural 4G penetration is only ~45%. App store downloads fail. 300M+ feature phones rely on USSD/2G, and field ASHA workers face zero-connectivity dead zones.

App Store Dependency: Excludes 55%+

4. Out-of-Pocket Drain

Out-of-pocket expenditure accounts for 62% of healthcare spend. Patients delay care due to wage loss & transit costs, converting simple fevers into sepsis.

Average Lost Wages: ₹400 / trip

The Core Insight: Digital health in India fails not because patients lack problems, but because solutions demand English literacy, high bandwidth, and complex mobile app installations.

Clinical Rigor

WHO IMCI Deterministic Clinical Protocols

Targeting India's highest-burden acute rural illness clusters with validated, auditable decision trees.

01 Cluster

Fever & Malaria

Tracks fever duration, temperature spikes, stiff neck, rash, convulsions, and malaria endemic zones.

• Score 1: Mild (<3d, no stiff neck)
• Score 2: Moderate (>3d or vomiting)
• Score 3: Meningitis / Convulsion
02 Cluster

Respiratory Distress

Evaluates cough duration, chest indrawing (ribcage sinking), stridor in calm child, and respiratory rate.

• Score 1: Simple Cold / Cough
• Score 2: Fast Breathing (Pneumonia)
• Score 3: Severe Chest Indrawing
03 Cluster

Diarrhoea & Dehydration

Analyzes stool frequency, blood in stool (dysentery), sunken eyes, skin pinch turgor, and thirst state.

• Score 1: No Dehydration (ORS)
• Score 2: Some Dehydration / Blood
• Score 3: Severe Shock / Lethargy
04 Cluster

Maternal Danger Signs

Assesses pregnant mothers for severe headaches, visual blurring, vaginal bleeding, and reduced fetal movement.

• Score 2: Reduced fetal movement
• Score 3: Pre-eclampsia / Hemorrhage
• Emergency 108 Auto-Dial Routing

Defense Matrix

Executive Q&A Defense Strategy

Bulletproof answers to tough technical, clinical, and regulatory inquiries.

Question: "How do you prevent LLM medical hallucinations?"

Response: "We do NOT use generative LLMs for medical reasoning. Sarvam AI is used strictly for translation and entity extraction. Medical triage relies 100% on deterministic decision trees executing official WHO IMCI protocols. Every outcome is reproducible, auditable, and rule-bounded."

Question: "How can illiterate rural citizens use this?"

Response: "Patients interact verbally via voice notes without viewing screens. For ASHA workers, the tablet PWA incorporates a Touch-to-Hear UX framework where every visual icon plays audio explanations in the local dialect upon tapping. Zero typing is required."

Question: "Is this classified as a CDSCO Medical Device?"

Response: "SwaraSetu operates as a clinical decision-support and navigation tool, not a diagnostic or prescriptive device. It incorporates medical disclaimers, routes users to accredited NHM PHCs, and integrates national helpline 104 as a fallback."

Question: "How does offline mode work without AI APIs?"

Response: "We use a dual-engine architecture. Patient WhatsApp runs online via Sarvam Cloud. The field-level ASHA Tablet PWA functions 100% offline using client-side JavaScript IMCI logic, pre-seeded PHC data, and pre-cached audio clips."

SwaraSetu (स्वर सेतु) — Product Requirements & Engineering Specification

Comprehensive synthesis of PRD, WHO IMCI clinical decision trees, Sarvam AI Indic integration points, and engineering execution plans.

1. Executive Summary

SwaraSetu is an offline-capable, voice-first triage assistant designed for India's last-mile healthcare gap. It enables patients and community health workers (CHWs) in rural and semi-urban settings to report symptoms in their native language — by voice or text — and receive an evidence-based triage outcome: self-care guidance, a CHW follow-up alert, or an urgent referral with the nearest Primary Health Centre (PHC) location.

The core differentiator is language-first design powered by Sarvam AI's Indic language stack (22+ Indian languages), combined with the WHO IMCI decision protocol adapted for India's NHM context. It requires no smartphone app, no English literacy, and no persistent internet connection — operating across WhatsApp, IVRS, USSD, and an offline-capable PWA on CHW tablets.
